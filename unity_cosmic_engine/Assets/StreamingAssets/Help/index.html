01 / FIRST FLIGHT

Take a look around.

  1. Launch Astronautica from the Start menu. You begin in the solar system.
  2. Choose a target in Navigation. Drag the open viewport to orbit it; use the mouse wheel to move closer.
  3. Try sectors 1–4, or press T for a narrated tour.
  4. Press S for the observatory. Open Sky → Change observer location before using local coordinates.
  5. Press G to browse telescope photographs. Search for M42 to explore the Orion Nebula.

Lost your bearings? Press R to restore the current view. This guide is always available with F1 or Settings → Help & field guide.

The guide opens in your default browser and works offline. Switch back to the app with the taskbar or Alt + Tab. External source links require the internet.

03 / FOUR COSMIC SCALES

Distance changes everything.

01 · Solar system
Sun, Earth, Jupiter, Saturn and Neptune. The 60.14 AU benchmark is Neptune’s orbital diameter. Body sizes and orbital spacing are schematic.
02 · Milky Way
NASA/JPL’s annotated artist map, with our Sun in the Orion Spur between Sagittarius and Perseus, about 26,000 light-years from the center. Click the gold Sun beacon or its label to return to the solar system; dragging still orbits. Select Orion Spur to inspect our neighborhood. The transit benchmark is 100,000 light-years.
03 · Local Group
A schematic of the three major spirals: Milky Way, Andromeda (M31) and Triangulum (M33). Select their labels or focus controls to inspect them. Dwarf galaxies are not modeled. Sizes, spacing and orientations are illustrative; the 10-million-light-year transit benchmark is independent of the diagram. M33 uses a telescope photograph.
04 · Cosmic web
An illustrative filament layer and horizon boundary, with a 93-billion-light-year benchmark. It is not a complete cosmological simulation.

Travel time and light pulses

Times compare distance ÷ speed in the observer’s frame. They do not include acceleration, relativistic traveler time or cosmic expansion. A pulse compresses its journey into ten playback seconds at 1×; the numerical light-travel time appears separately. Use its pause and playback-speed controls to inspect progress.

The tour advances through the sectors after narration and a short viewing interval. Press T again to return to free exploration.

04 / YOUR LOCAL SKY

A horizon you can navigate.

Press S for the observatory. The horizon, compass directions and level camera establish your orientation. Drag to look around, use the direction buttons to face a cardinal point, and use the wheel to adjust the telescope’s field of view.

Set your observer location

  1. Open the Sky instrument and select Change observer location.
  2. Choose an observatory preset, or enter a custom latitude (−90 to +90) and longitude (−180 to +180) in decimal degrees.
  3. Use positive numbers for north/east and negative numbers for south/west. Apply the location.

San Marcos, TX is included in the location presets. Select it to use the city’s approximate reference coordinates (29.8736° N, 97.9367° W). For a specific observing site, enter custom coordinates.

The starting preset is Mauna Kea, not your detected location. Coordinates are entered locally; GPS is not requested. Positions use the computer’s current UTC time, so keep the Windows date and time correct.

Read altitude and azimuth

N · 0°E · 90°S · 180°W · 270°

Altitude measures angle above the horizon: 0° is the horizon and +90° is overhead. Negative altitude means the target is below the horizon. Azimuth measures direction clockwise from north.

Select an above-horizon catalog target to aim at it. The catalog can display visible targets or all targets. Below-horizon objects remain available for reading and photographs, but cannot be located above your current horizon. Toggle constellation lines, horizon, markers and star labels independently.

Coordinates are approximate, based on J2000 positions and current sidereal time without precession or atmospheric refraction. This is an orientation aid, not precision telescope guidance. The backdrop and landscape are decorative; daylight, weather and real-world obstructions are not modeled.

05 / TELESCOPE GALLERY

Real light. Distant worlds.

All 110 Messier objects and 12 selected deep-sky highlights have photographs bundled locally. Open G, then search by common name, M/NGC identifier, constellation abbreviation or object type. Use All, Messier, Highlights or Above horizon to narrow the collection.

Select a photograph to see its full image, description, current altitude/azimuth and exact source credit. Locate in the sky takes you to the observatory when the object is above the horizon. The original source and usage links open your browser. Press Esc to close the gallery.

The highlights include the North America, Helix, Carina, Western Veil, Bubble, Rosette and Tarantula nebulae; NGC 2392; 47 Tucanae; Omega Centauri; Centaurus A; and NGC 869 in the Double Cluster.

Some images show a detail or surrounding field, and color may represent scientific filters. M102 uses the traditional, historically disputed identification NGC 5866. This gallery is a curated selection beyond Messier, not every known deep-sky object.

06 / SOUND & ACCESSIBILITY

Set your own pace.

Open Settings for text scale, high contrast and units. Both side instruments scroll and can collapse. H cycles quieter views when you want more space for the scene. The interface fits smaller windows by scaling the complete console; a 1280 × 720 window or larger is recommended.

The player has nine instrumentals, including five three-minute ambient pieces: Asterism, Drift Beyond Orion, Ion Trails, Pale Blue Horizon and The Long Return. Use Pause/Play, Next track, Shuffle and the volume slider. Music crossfades and softens during narration. Mute all audio includes music, narration and effects; Pause music affects the soundtrack only.

Text scale, contrast, units, observer location, music volume and shuffle are saved locally. Narration, mute and playback state are session controls.

07 / INSTALL & UPDATE

Bring the observatory home.

Use Windows 10 (21H1 or later) or Windows 11, 64-bit, with a DirectX 11-capable graphics device. Reserve about 500 MB for the installed app and temporary setup space. No Unity editor, account or network connection is needed to run the installed app.

  1. Download the setup EXE from the official GitHub release.
  2. Run setup and choose the install folder. The default is %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Astronautica; administrator access is not required.
  3. Optionally create a desktop shortcut. Launch from the Start menu or setup’s final page.

The installer is unsigned, so Windows may show an unknown-publisher warning. Downloads include SHA256SUMS.txt for integrity checking. Obtain releases from the repository above; do not disable Windows security settings.

Portable edition

Extract the entire ZIP into a writable folder and run CosmicZoomEngine.exe. Keep its CosmicZoomEngine_Data, MonoBleedingEdge, DLLs and other bundled files together. Copying only the EXE will not work.

Updates and removal

Close Astronautica before installing a newer release into the same location. There is no automatic updater. For a portable update, extract into a new folder. Uninstall the installed edition through Windows Settings → Apps or the Start menu uninstaller. Local preferences and Unity player logs are retained when uninstalling.

08 / TROUBLESHOOTING

Find your way back.

The scene is off screen or unfamiliar.
Close overlays and press R. Use S to leave the observatory or 1 to return to the solar system. Use H if instruments are hidden.
My object is below the horizon.
Check the selected observer location and computer time. Switch to All targets to read about it or open its gallery photograph. A negative altitude is expected for an object below your local horizon.
There is no music.
In Settings, select Play music, unmute all audio and raise music volume. Check the Windows volume mixer/output device. Narration temporarily lowers music volume.
The photograph or help page is missing.
Reinstall or extract the complete package. Both are stored in CosmicZoomEngine_Data\StreamingAssets. They do not download during normal use.
The app does not start or has rendering problems.
Keep all runtime files together, install current graphics drivers from your hardware manufacturer, and try a smaller window. Include your Windows version and graphics card when reporting a problem.
Where are logs and preferences?
Unity writes logs to %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\DefaultCompany\Astronautica - Cosmic Explorer\Player.log. Preferences are in HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\DefaultCompany\Astronautica - Cosmic Explorer. Review logs before posting them publicly because they can contain machine details. Settings can normally be changed inside the app.

Report reproducible problems at GitHub Issues. Include the release version, steps, expected result and actual result. Screenshots help explain visual problems.
